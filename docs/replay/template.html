Fuel-optimal convex guidance versus a braking heuristic for tower capture

A simulation study of catching a Super Heavy booster with the launch tower's arms.

Aiden Huang · October 2026

Overview

In the last 16 seconds of a Super Heavy return, the booster has to arrive at the tower's catch arms within a metre, descending at walking pace. This study compares two ways of flying that burn in simulation: a convex model-predictive controller that re-solves a fuel-optimal trajectory every second, and an engineered braking-profile controller. Both share the same sensors, engines, estimator, terminal descent and abort logic, so only the guidance differs.

Across 300 dispersed landing burns with wind, gusts, model errors and random engine failures, the convex controller catches more boosters, more precisely, with more propellant to spare. Its weak point is a mid-burn engine failure, where braking late leaves less room to divert. A speed cap near the tower and an engine-out braking reserve address it. Vehicle figures are public estimates, so the comparisons and mechanisms are the result rather than the absolute numbers.
